3. Edits, features and priority

After your site is live you can keep asking for changes. There is no monthly allowance and no per-change fee: edits and new features are included on every plan.

  • An edit is a change to existing pages: wording, images, design, prices, structure.
  • A feature is something new that did not exist before: a booking form, a gallery, an extra page with new functionality.
  • The distinction only affects how long the work takes, never what it costs.

Edits on Starter. On Starter you can ask for edits to what is already on your site — wording, images, prices, opening hours, contact details and links — whenever you need them. There is no limit on how many you send or how often, and no charge for any of them. We work through them in the order they arrive, as quickly as we reasonably can. Starter has no set turnaround time, and Business and One requests are handled first (see Priority below). Something new that the site does not already do, such as online bookings, payments, live chat or an extra form, is a feature, and features are part of Business.

Priority. Requests are worked through one at a time for each site, One requests first, then Business. Then Starter. Within a plan, the request waiting longest goes next. Edits are included on every plan, and new features on Business and One, at no extra charge; a feature we build for one customer may be offered to others afterwards (section 10 covers who owns what). One buys a place at the front of the queue plus its own extras — monthly search and site improvements, a business email inbox, automated customer emails, and advertising run for you. One is built as a model for a business type (for example Trades, Clubs or Salon); which model applies is agreed when you join, and the same price applies whatever the type.

Advertising on One. On One we set up, run and monitor advertising for your business on Facebook and Instagram. Who pays Meta for the ads is set out under “Ads on One” in section 6: when we fund them, we pay Meta ourselves, up to £100 a month; when you fund them, you pay Meta directly on your own ad account and can change or pause the budget at any time, and we never hold or spend your advertising money. We need admin access to your Facebook page and ad account, and at least a photo or a line of text from you, before anything can run. We write the offer and the ad, build the form or page it leads to, and send you a monthly note saying what ran, what it cost and what came in. We aim to have your first ad live at the start of month 2 of your plan, once your site is built and tested and we have the access and material we need from you; Meta’s own review of a new ad account or a new ad can take longer, and that part is not ours to control. This is work we do with reasonable skill and care, not a promise of any number of leads, sales or a cost per lead (section 2). Meta’s own advertising policies apply and may stop an ad running; where they do, we tell you and rework it. We run advertising for a limited number of businesses at a time so each one gets proper attention; the number and the current price are shown on the plans page. A price shown to you when you joined One stays your price while you remain on it.

Fair use. Unlimited means you are never charged for asking; it does not mean parallel teams working simultaneously. We do requests in order, at a pace that keeps every customer’s work moving. If a run of requests is large enough that it would crowd out other customers, we may schedule it across a longer period, and we will tell you when to expect what.

Any live tool we set up for you to run yourself — a menu, your prices, a timetable — is always free for you to change instantly, without asking us at all.

We decide in good faith whether a request is an edit or a feature, and how requests are ordered within the rules above. If you disagree, tell us and we will explain the reasoning.

4. Getting your site built

Starter: live within 24 hours. When you join Starter, we build your site from our designs around your business, using the page you sent us when you joined (your Instagram, Facebook or current site), register your web address (or point one you already own) and put it live within 24 hours. Registries and DNS are run by third parties; where one of them delays, we put it live as soon as it clears and tell you.

Business: ready in 7 to 14 days. When you join Business, we build your site and the features you chose when you signed up (up to three), register your web address (or point one you already own), and set up what those features need, such as card payments and email. It is ready within 14 days of joining, often sooner. On Starter the site is built from our designs around your business, with a contact form and click to call, and you can ask for edits from day one. Anything you ask for after that, on either plan, is built in the usual way, in turn, under section 3. If your Business site is not ready within 14 days, your next month is free: we credit one month of your plan to your account, applied by Stripe against your next payment. The credit is not paid in cash and does not apply where a plan is cancelled before the credit is used.

The 14 days pause while we are waiting on something we have asked you for — photos, prices, wording, or your approval of a change — and restart when it arrives. We will always ask before the clock stops, and chase before it matters.

One: your website and app. When you join One we send you a setup form about your business. Your website is ready 7 to 14 days after you complete it, often sooner; anything you leave out, we ask about, and the build waits for the answer where it needs it. We build your member app and submit it to the App Store and Google Play as soon as your own developer accounts are ready (section 6). How long Apple and Google take to review and approve it is up to them, not us: usually 1 to 4 weeks, sometimes longer, and either of them may ask for changes first. Until it is approved, you and your members use the same features on your website. Apple asks a business for a D-U-N-S number (a free company identifier) before it opens a developer account, which can take a week or two, so we ask you to start your accounts when you join. The 24 hours, the 14 days and the free month above are for Starter and Business; they do not apply to One.

If we cannot start because we are waiting on something from you, the plan still runs and is still billed.

7. Changing plan

You can change plan at any time from your account.

Moving up

An upgrade takes effect immediately. Stripe prorates it: you pay the difference between the two plans for the rest of your current month, not a fresh full month. Your requests move up the queue straight away.

Moving down

A downgrade takes effect at your next payment date. You keep the plan you have paid for until then, and there is no refund for the unused part of the month. Your queue position moves when the new plan starts.

8. Cancelling

You can cancel at any time. No further payments are taken after you cancel, and the service runs to the end of the period you have already paid for — the month, or on an annual plan the year. Nothing is locked in, and there is no exit fee.

8.1 To cancel, use the billing portal from your account, or email us. Cancelling in Stripe and telling us are equivalent; you do not need to do both.

8.2 When the period you have paid for ends — the month, or on an annual plan the year — we take the site offline and stop the plan.

8.3 What you take with you. On request we send you an export of your content and data — the text, images and business information you gave us, and anything your site collected for you, such as enquiries and customer details — and we transfer your web address to you with its transfer code, free of charge (section 9). The site itself — the design, pages, features and code — is part of the service rather than something you buy, so it stops with the plan and is not handed over. Section 11 says more about who owns what.

8.4 Six months to change your mind. We keep your site build and account data for six months after your plan ends. Restart within that time and everything picks up where it left off — the same site, the same features, usually back online within days. If the account stays unused for six months, the build and the data are permanently deleted, and coming back later means starting a fresh build.

8.5 Payments already taken are not refunded, including where you cancel part way through a billing period. The credit under section 4 is a credit against a future payment, not a refund. This applies to annual plans in the same way: cancelling part way through a year does not refund the remaining months — your site simply stays online and looked after until the year you paid for ends.

8.6 If you are a consumer

If you are taking a plan as an individual rather than for business purposes, the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 give you 14 days from signing up to cancel for any reason. Because we start the build as soon as you sign up, you are asking us to begin during that period; if you then cancel within the 14 days, we may charge you a proportionate amount for the work already done. This right is in addition to, not instead of, your right to cancel at any time above.
