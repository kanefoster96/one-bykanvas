Free trial or paid trial: which is better for a kids’ club?

Every decision a parent makes comes down to how risky it feels. Why we use free trials, and when a paid trial can work instead.

Kane Foster, founder of Kanvas One and owner of Kanvas Academy in Newcastle
4 min read · Updated 19 October 2026

It’s one of the first decisions every club makes. Do you let children try a class for free, or charge for the first one?

Both can work. Which is right for you comes down to one thing: how much risk the parent feels.

Every decision comes down to risk

Whenever a parent is deciding whether to try your club, they’re quietly asking themselves how much is at stake. What if my child hates it? What if it’s not as good as it looks? What if I’ve paid and we never go back?

The bigger that feels, the fewer parents take the first step. It’s called perceived risk, and it decides more bookings than your price, your photos or your timetable.

Business wordPerceived risk

How risky a decision feels to the person making it: the money, the time, an upset child, the awkwardness of leaving. The lower it feels, the more people say yes. A free trial makes it close to nothing.

Why we choose free trials

A free trial takes the risk away. The parent has paid nothing. If their child doesn’t like it, they don’t have to join, and nobody has lost anything. That makes it an easy yes.

And once a child has been in the room, met the coach and had a great time, the class does the selling for you.

When a paid trial can work

Paid trials aren’t wrong. They can work well for a club with a very strong reputation and a waiting list, where parents have already decided they want to join. For them, paying for the first class is just a formality.

The problem is everyone else. A parent who isn’t set on your club yet is comparing options. If the club down the road offers a free trial and you charge, they’ll often try the free one first, because paying for something their child might not like is a risk they don’t have to take.

Free trial

The parent risks nothing

  • An easy yes, even for parents still comparing clubs
  • The class does the selling
  • Right for most clubs

Paid trial

The parent risks money

  • Puts off parents who aren’t sure yet
  • Fine when parents have already decided
  • Suits clubs with a waiting list

Take away the risk, and more families say yes. Then let the class do the selling.

Free doesn’t have to mean careless

The worry with free trials is people booking with no intention of coming. You can keep the risk low for parents and still get serious bookings:

  • Ask a question when they book. We ask what they want their child to get out of classes. It takes seconds, and it filters out casual bookings. That’s good friction.
  • Have them pick a date, rather than “come along some time”.
  • Remind them before the class. We send four messages before every trial, and 15 of our last 22 turned up.

A quick checklist

  1. Think about how risky the first step feels to a parent.
  2. Use free trials unless parents are already queuing to join.
  3. Ask a question at booking so the trials you get are serious.
  4. Remind every family before their trial.

Questions

Should my club offer a free trial or a paid trial?

For most clubs, a free trial. It takes the risk away for parents, so more of them take the first step. Paid trials can work for clubs with a strong reputation and a waiting list, where parents have already decided to join.

Won’t people book a free trial and not turn up?

Some will. Asking a question at booking, having them pick a date and sending reminders all help. 15 of our last 22 free trial bookings turned up.
