How to get more free trials to turn up at your kids’ club

Free trials book, then never arrive. The four messages we send before every trial, and why 15 of our last 22 trial bookings turned up.

Kane Foster, founder of Kanvas One and owner of Kanvas Academy in Newcastle
6 min read · Updated 19 October 2026

If you run a kids’ club, you’ll know this one. A parent books a free trial on Sunday night. You add the child to the register, you plan for them, and on the day nobody comes. No message, no reason.

It isn’t that the parent didn’t care. They booked because they wanted their child to try it. Somewhere between booking and the day itself, life got in the way.

Why free trials don’t turn up

Most no-shows come down to a handful of things, and none of them are about your club:

  • They booked on a whim. It felt like a great idea at 9pm on the sofa. By Thursday it’s just another thing on the list.
  • They forgot. The booking email went to an inbox they barely open.
  • The child got nervous. A new room full of children they don’t know is a big deal at six years old, and parents don’t want a battle at the door.
  • Free feels like nothing to lose. Nobody has paid, so skipping it doesn’t feel like letting anyone down.

You can’t fix any of those with a better class. You fix them with what happens between the booking and the door.

The four messages we send before every trial

At our own club, every trial booking gets four messages before the class. One confirms it, and three remind them, each timed for a reason.

1. The confirmation, straight away

The moment they book, they get an email with a trial pass and a QR code to show at the door. It makes the booking feel real, and it gives them something to come back to.

2. The evening before

A short text the night before. It lands while they’re planning tomorrow, which is exactly when they need it. Families who have your app get it as a notification instead, so nobody gets reminded twice.

3. 9am on the day

A quick “today’s the day” text, early enough to plan the afternoon around.

4. An hour before, with the coach’s name

This is the one that matters most, and it does almost nothing. It doesn’t remind them of the time or tell the child not to be nervous. It just says a named coach will be waiting at the door.

Text, 4:30pmCoach Jess will be at the door to meet Ava at 5:30pm.

That turns “we might pop along” into an appointment with a real person who is expecting them. A spot has been kept for their child, and someone will notice if they don’t come. People turn up for people.

Reminding people helps. Making them feel expected is what gets them through the door.

What to do when someone still doesn’t come

Some won’t, and that’s fine. The worst thing you can do is nothing, because a no-show is still a family who wanted to come.

  • The next day, offer another date. One friendly message with a link to rebook. No guilt, no “we missed you”.
  • If that goes quiet, ask why. A one-tap question: was it the day, the time, or something else? You’ll learn more from those answers than from any survey.
  • After a trial they did come to, follow up within a day. Ask how it went and send the link to join while it’s still fresh.

A quick checklist

  1. Send a confirmation the moment they book, with something to show at the door.
  2. Remind them the evening before.
  3. Remind them on the morning of the class.
  4. An hour before, tell them who will be waiting for them.
  5. Rebook no-shows the next day, and ask the quiet ones why.

You can do all of this by hand with a phone and a spreadsheet. It just has to happen every time, for every booking, including the ones made at 11pm.

Questions

How many reminders should I send before a free trial?

We send four messages: a confirmation straight after booking, a reminder the evening before, one at 9am on the day, and one an hour before that names the coach who will meet them. 15 of our last 22 trial bookings turned up.

Should trial reminders be texts or emails?

Both have a job. The confirmation works best as an email, because it carries the trial pass and QR code. The reminders work best as texts, because most people read a text within minutes. Families with your app can get notifications instead.

What should I do when a trial doesn’t turn up?

Message them the next day with a link to pick another date, without any guilt. If that goes quiet, ask with one tap what got in the way. Many families just had a busy week.
