How to start a kids’ club: pricing, rent and your first members

Thinking of starting a kids’ club? The basics you need in place, how to price it so the rent and coaches are covered, and how to get your first members.

Kane Foster, founder of Kanvas One and owner of Kanvas Academy in Newcastle
7 min read · Updated 19 October 2026

I started Kanvas Academy in 2017. If you’re thinking about starting a club of your own, this is what I’d want someone to tell me: what to sort out first, how to make sure the rent and coaches are covered, and how to get your first families through the door.

1. Decide what and who

Start narrow. One style, one age group, one evening. “Street dance for 5 to 8 year olds, Tuesdays” is far easier to fill and to explain than “dance for everyone”. You can add classes once the first one is full.

2. Check what you need to work with children

Insurance, background checks and policies for working with children all need sorting before your first class. I’m not the expert on the rules, so ask your sport’s governing body what applies to you.

3. Find a hall

School halls, community centres, church halls and leisure centres are all worth asking. Most charge by the hour. Start with one or two evenings, and ask about holidays and whether you can store kit.

4. Work out your price before day one

This is where a lot of new clubs go wrong. They pick a low price to get started, then find the rent and the coach take most of it.

Work it out the other way round: add up your rent, your coaches, your insurance and what you want to take home for the year. Divide by 10 months, then by 80% of your spaces. That’s your price. We’ve written up exactly how to do the sum, with an example.

And don’t be afraid of a proper price. When I charged £16 a month, it was very quiet. At £50 we were close to fully booked.

5. Get your first members

  • Offer a free trial. It’s the easiest yes for a parent who has never heard of you.
  • Make it easy to find you. A simple website with your class, ages, day, time and area, and a Google Business Profile.
  • Reach local parents. A small budget of local Facebook and Instagram ads goes a long way. Our first four weeks cost £145 and brought in 24 trial bookings.
  • Make sure trials turn up. Confirm and remind every booking. We send four messages before every trial.
  • Make joining take one tap. Send the link to join the same day as the trial.

6. Start the way you mean to go on

It’s tempting to start with cash, paper forms and a WhatsApp group, and sort it out later. Later never comes, and by then you have fifty families to move. Card payments, online booking and proper registers from day one save you hours every week.

Questions

What do I need to start a kids’ club?

Decide what you’ll teach and to which ages, check with your sport’s governing body what you need for working with children, find a hall, and work out your price before your first class. Then offer free trials to get your first members.

How much should I charge when I start?

Work it out from your costs. Add up rent, coaches, insurance and what you want to take home for the year, divide by 10 months, then divide by 80% of your spaces. Don’t start too low: we were very quiet at £16 a month and close to full at £50.

How do I get my first members?

Offer a free trial, set up a simple website and Google Business Profile, reach local parents with a small budget of local ads, and confirm and remind every trial booking so they turn up.
