Kids’ club management software: what you actually need

Spreadsheets, WhatsApp and bank transfers, or one system? What a kids’ club really needs to manage trials, members, payments and registers, and the questions to ask before you choose.

Kane Foster, founder of Kanvas One and owner of Kanvas Academy in Newcastle
6 min read · Updated 18 November 2026

Most clubs start the same way: a spreadsheet for members, a WhatsApp group for parents, bank transfers you check by hand and a paper register. It works until it doesn’t, usually around the time your evenings disappear. Here’s what a kids’ club actually needs to manage, and how to choose software for it.

What you’re really managing

  • Trials: who’s booked, who turned up, and who to follow up so they join.
  • Members: who’s in which class, their medical notes and emergency contacts.
  • Payments: monthly fees taken on time, failed payments chased, siblings and pauses handled.
  • Registers: who’s in tonight, without paper.
  • Messages: class cancelled, show tickets, kit orders, without your personal number.
  • New families: being found by parents searching for a club near them, and turning interest into trials.

Most club software only does the middle of that list. The first and last items are where your growth comes from.

The three usual setups

  1. Spreadsheets, WhatsApp and bank transfers. Free, and fine for a handful of families. It costs you your evenings, and payments slip because nobody likes chasing friends for money.
  2. Booking software. Takes bookings and payments, often for a monthly fee plus a percentage of what parents pay. It runs the classes you already have, but finding new families is still up to you.
  3. An all-in-one system run for you. Your own app and website, bookings, payments and registers, plus ads and Google work to bring new families in. Costs more each month, but replaces several things you’d otherwise pay for separately.

Questions to ask before you choose

  • Do you take a percentage of my payments? 1 to 2.5% sounds small, but it grows with your club.
  • Is the app mine? Your club’s name in the App Store, or a shared app with other clubs in it?
  • Who does the switch-over? Moving members, memberships and your timetable is the part that puts most owners off.
  • Can parents book a trial and join without messaging me? Every message you don’t have to answer is time back.
  • Does it help me get new members, or just manage the ones I have?
  • Who do I talk to when something’s wrong? A real person, or a ticket queue?

The right software should give you your evenings back and fill your empty spaces. If it only does one, you’ll still be paying for the other somewhere else.

Questions

What is the best software to manage a kids’ club?

The one that runs your admin and helps you get new members. Look for trial bookings, monthly payments with failed-payment chasing, registers on your phone, your own app, and no percentage of your payments.

Is there free software for managing a kids’ club?

Some booking tools are free to start but take a percentage of every payment, so they cost more as you grow. Spreadsheets and WhatsApp are free but cost your evenings.

How do I switch club software without losing members?

Start new families on the new system and move existing members across when you’re ready, ideally between terms, with one message to parents explaining the change. With Kanvas One, we do the move for you.
